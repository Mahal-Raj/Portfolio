01 / A WORKING PHILOSOPHY

I enjoy understanding how systems connect rather than learning tools in isolation.

I follow a request beyond the interface: into the network, through the delivery pipeline, across the infrastructure and down to the operating system that makes it possible.

My goal is not to collect tools. I want enough context to ask better questions when something changes, slows down or breaks.

02 / HOW I READ A SYSTEM

Start with the experience.
Keep following the signal.

I learn fastest when I can connect an idea to the complete path it travels.

01

The request

I start with the visible behaviour: what needs to happen, who needs it and what a useful result looks like.

02

The path

I trace the network, permissions and dependencies that let the request move between systems.

03

The runtime

I look at the operating system, container and cloud resources carrying the workload.

04

The evidence

I use health checks, logs and reproducible steps to understand whether the system is doing what I intended.

03 / HOW I THINK

Simple principles.
Useful under pressure.

  1. 01

04 / HOW I SHOW UP

The habits behind
the technical work.

Good systems work is not only configuration. It is the quality of the questions, decisions and explanations around it.

01

Calm troubleshooting

I try to replace urgency with a clear sequence: observe, narrow the scope, test an assumption and keep notes.

02

Reproducible decisions

I prefer configuration and documented steps that someone else can review, repeat and improve.

03

Useful communication

Teaching, research and support work taught me to explain the system without hiding behind jargon.

04

Connected thinking

I treat software, infrastructure and networks as parts of one experience rather than separate boxes.
